1The basics

What is The Lede?

A read-later app for iPhone with a research desk behind it. Save an article from anywhere; The Lede reads the whole piece, unburies what it's actually about, renames it plainly, files it by beat, and writes a short brief: the strongest honest case the piece makes. You're the editor. The Lede is your research desk.

What's a "brief"?

What the desk hands back for every article you save: a plain rename of the piece by its real argument, a beat (the topic desk it's filed under), and a few sentences making the strongest honest version of the case the article is arguing. Briefs are also the unit your free monthly allowance counts.

Why did my saved article come back with a different name?

Because the headline you clicked and the story underneath it aren't always the same thing. The Lede reads the whole piece and renames it for what it's actually about, so your queue reads like what you saved, not like what got you to click. It never touches the original: every renamed story carries a "RAN AS" line showing the real headline, exactly as it ran. Nothing is swapped behind your back; you can always see both.

What's Dig Deeper?

A deeper pass you can ask for on a story you care about. The desk goes out and researches it, pulls in additional reporting and other angles, and writes a richer read: the tension in the story, a few different takes, and an honest bottom line. It works in the background and lets you know when it's ready. Dig Deeper is part of the paid plans, and your first two are free so you can see what it does.

2Saving articles

How do I save an article?

From Safari or almost any app: tap Share, then choose The Lede. The article uploads in the background and the brief lands in your queue when it's ready. Usually that's quick, and it keeps working even if you leave the app.

Can I save from Chrome, Brave, Firefox, or apps like Reddit?

Yes, from almost anywhere the Share button appears. When you share from Safari, The Lede reads the page exactly as you see it, signed in and all. From other browsers and apps it receives the link and reads the article on its own. If a page can't be read that way, the save is still kept; you'll see a card telling you exactly how to finish it (see the locked-door question below).

I subscribe to a publication. Can I save its articles?

Yes. Share them from Safari, where you're already signed in. The desk reads the page in your own logged-in session, so it reads exactly what you can read: nothing more, nothing bypassed. If a story can't be reached that way, The Lede tells you and keeps the save.

I got a card that says "Locked door." What do I do?

That card means the desk couldn't get into the article on its own: usually a site that wants a signed-in reader, or a link shared from outside Safari. Tap the card, then Open in Safari: the article opens inside The Lede, signed in as you. Read it, tap Share, and send it back to The Lede from there. The desk reads exactly what you can read, and the save stays in your queue either way.

A brief didn't show up. Where is it?

Uploads finish in the background, so a brief can land a moment after you've moved on. Check your connection and reopen The Lede; anything waiting will finish. If a save genuinely failed, it shows as a card that says so, never silence.

3Your queue, briefs & reading

Where are my saved articles?

In your queue. Once you've read one it moves to your Archive. The menu in the queue header takes you there. Nothing is deleted unless you delete it.

What are beats?

The desks your stories are filed under: politics, technology, science, business, culture, world, design, health. The Lede files each save by what it's about, so your library reads like sections, not a pile.

Can I read the original article?

Always. The original is saved with the brief and rendered cleanly for reading, and there's a link to the source. The brief sits above the article, never instead of it.

Can I highlight and take notes?

Yes. Select text in a brief or a Dig Deeper read and highlight it, with a note if you want. Your highlights live in their own collection and sync with everything else.

Can I share a brief or send it to my notes app?

Yes. From the reader you can share a short rename-and-reveal (the plain headline and what the piece is really about) using the standard share sheet, and you can export a brief as Markdown into your own notes app, like Obsidian, for personal use.

4Free & paid plans

What's free?

Your first 10 briefs every month: no account, no card, no trial clock. The clock resets monthly. Your first two Dig Deepers are also free, so you can see the desk at full stretch before deciding anything.

What happens if I save more than 10 articles in a month?

Every save is kept: nothing is ever lost or turned away. Past 10, a save stays buried: filed in your queue, original readable, but its brief withheld. Upgrade any time and the desk goes back and unburies everything you've saved.

What are the paid plans?

Two. Lede+ ($7.99/month, with a 7-day free trial): unlimited briefs and 25 Dig Deepers a month. Lede Pro ($14.99/month): unlimited briefs and unlimited Dig Deeper. Unlimited covers normal personal reading, and both plans carry fair-use safeguards against automated or abusive volume. In rare stretches of exceptional demand, new briefs may be paused briefly; this rarely affects everyday reading.

How does the free trial work?

Lede+ starts with 7 days free: unlimited briefs from the first minute, plus 3 Dig Deeper investigations during the trial. When the trial ends it automatically becomes a paid subscription at $7.99/month and the full 25-per-month Dig Deeper allowance begins, unless you cancel first, in which case you pay nothing. You can cancel any time in Settings → your name → Subscriptions.

How do I subscribe, change plans, or cancel?

Everything runs through Apple. Subscribe or move between Lede+ and Pro inside the app; manage or cancel any time in Settings → your name → Subscriptions → The Lede on your iPhone. The Lede never sees your card; Apple handles all of it.

I subscribed but the app still treats me as free.

Tap Restore Purchases on the subscription screen; that also brings an active subscription to a new iPhone. If it still doesn't take, email support@theledeapp.com with the purchase date and we'll sort it out.

5Privacy & your data

Do I need an account? What do you collect?

No account: no name, no password, and the app never asks for your email. Your library lives in your own iCloud. We collect a small amount of usage data, keyed to a random per-device identifier rather than your name, to run the service and set fair pricing. No ads, no third-party trackers, nothing sold. The exact details are in the Privacy Policy.

What happens to an article's text when I save it?

It's sent to our server just long enough to write the brief: passed to our AI provider, Anthropic, never used to train AI models, and never stored. What we keep is limited and self-deleting: a short, non-reversible fingerprint of the text so the same article isn't processed twice, the brief itself for a brief window, and the article's web address (never its text). The Privacy Policy has the binding detail, including every provider involved.

Do you keep a record of what I read?

Not a readable, identity-linked one. Your saved articles, briefs, and highlights live in your own iCloud, never on our servers. To run the service we keep limited usage signals (always tied to a random per-device identifier, never your name), such as a story's publication and a non-reversible fingerprint of its address. We don't keep a readable list of the specific articles you read. The Privacy Policy spells out exactly what's retained.

Can I delete my data?

Yes. Deleting the app and its iCloud data removes your library. Email support@theledeapp.com and we'll delete the usage records tied to your device. We may keep one Apple-issued transaction identifier tied to a recent subscription where we need it to handle refunds and billing disputes; it carries no name or payment details. Full detail in the Privacy Policy.

6Sources & standards

Does The Lede block or favor certain outlets?

No outlet is blocked, filtered, or down-ranked for who it is. The desk holds one editorial standard: steel-man everything, applied to every source the same way. The full standard is written down in How The Lede sources.

What happens when I save an opinion piece?

The desk briefs the argument at its strongest, like anything else. When a brief needs the underlying reporting (what actually happened, beyond the columnist's account), it reads from solid accounts of the same events and says so in the brief. Nothing is swapped silently, and it works the same for every outlet's opinion pages alike.

Is it politically biased?

Its job is the opposite of a take: make the strongest honest case for what you saved, whatever its viewpoint. There's no list of approved or banned outlets; there's a standard, published for anyone to read at How The Lede sources.

7Devices & sync

Does it sync?

Your library (articles, briefs, highlights) lives in your iCloud and follows your Apple ID. Delete the app and reinstall, and your library comes back.

Is there an Android or web version?

No. The Lede is an iPhone app.

8Accessibility

Is The Lede accessible?

It's built to be. By rule, not by roadmap. The Lede is designed and tested against WCAG 2.2 AA, the recognized standard for accessible software, and here an accessibility gap is a bug that blocks a release. The app works with VoiceOver and Switch Control, scales through the largest accessibility text sizes, and quiets its animation when Reduce Motion is on. And it's checked the way it's actually used: with the assistive technology switched on, on a real iPhone, not just in code review.

I read with VoiceOver or large text. What should I expect?

The same magazine, not a side entrance. Cards read as one clean announcement each, with their actions (open, archive, try again) in the rotor instead of buried in unlabeled buttons. Saves and status changes are announced. Briefs and articles reflow at every text size, and nothing meaningful is carried by color alone. The same-experience rule cuts both ways: when a brief is buried past the free allowance, it's withheld from every reader the same way; a screen reader is never handed text a sighted reader can't see. Highlights are part of that: create one straight from the actions rotor, hear each one announced with its note as you read, and open, edit, or delete it. All verified on a real iPhone with VoiceOver running.

Something doesn't work with my setup. How do I tell you?

Email support@theledeapp.com and mention what you were using: VoiceOver, Switch Control, larger text, a braille display, anything. Accessibility reports jump the queue: they're filed as blocking bugs and stay open until the fix is verified with the same assistive technology you reported it with. This site holds itself to the same standard: if a page here doesn't read right, that's a bug too.

9When something goes wrong

A save failed or a site won't read. What now?

The save is kept either way: a failed read shows as a card, and most fix with the open-in-Safari, share-it-back move (see the "Locked door" answer above). If a particular site never works, tell us: support@theledeapp.com. Fixing sources is ongoing desk work.

How do I reach a human?

support@theledeapp.com. We're a small shop; we aim to reply within a couple of days.
