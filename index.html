A short film about The Lede
Silent · 79 seconds

The film, line by line

The film sets its words on paper, one line at a time, and a simple drawing acts out each one. A single orange dot stands for the lede.

  1. Everything comes at us at once. Gray dots pour in from every edge until the page is crowded.
  2. How do we make sense of any of it? The dots jostle. None settles.
  3. We save it to make sense of later. A few drop into a pile at the bottom.
  4. Later never comes. The pile keeps growing and grays over.
  5. So we live on headlines. A heavy black bar lands on the pile, and everything under it blurs.
  6. And hot takes that thrive on outrage. The bar cracks into shards that spin faster until the page goes dark.
  7. The Lede slows us down. In the dark, the spinning stops and the shards lie flat as quiet lines.
  8. It reads all of it. A slim marker moves along every line.
  9. It unburies the lede. The first orange dot rises through the lines.
  10. So we know if it’s worth our time. The heavy bar shrinks to a short plain line beside the dot.
  11. Sometimes it isn’t. The lines slide off the page. The dot holds, then fades.
  12. When it is, we dig deeper. A new orange dot drops one fine thread down.
  13. It looks in the research and the reporting. Threads fan out to gray dots at the edges.
  14. And lists every source. Each far dot gets a number, and the threads pull taut.
  15. Hear the author out. On a dark page, the orange dot alone.
  16. Then see what the sources say. One by one, light runs along each thread from a far dot back to the orange dot.
  17. And decide for ourselves. The threads let go. One orange dot, still.
  18. The Lede · Check everything you read. The name on paper.

Dig Deeper

When it is, dig deeper.

Every brief starts by hearing the author out: the strongest honest case the piece makes. When a piece is worth more of your time, tap Dig Deeper. The desk looks in the research and the reporting around it, sets out what others found and where the desk lands, and lists every source it wrote from, each one a link to the original. It can get one wrong, so check the ones that matter to you. Run it on a news story, a how-to, or the history thread you fell into at midnight.

A note on sourcing

It finds the reporting under the opinion.

The Lede reads every source as it is. It doesn't rank outlets and it reads every one. The one thing it changes: when a piece is opinion, it builds the brief from the original reporting underneath, and shows you the reporting it used. Same rule for every publication.
