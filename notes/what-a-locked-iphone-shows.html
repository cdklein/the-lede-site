What a locked iPhone shows of your reading

Three changes, and what they don’t cover.

What you save says a lot about you. So we went through what The Lede shows to someone holding your iPhone while it’s locked, and changed three things.

Two Shortcuts actions ask you to unlock first. In version 1.0.4 and later, the Shortcuts actions that read your latest briefs or save an article ask you to unlock your iPhone before they run. Our own review caught this before those actions were released in 1.0.4, so no App Store version of The Lede let them run on a locked phone.

Article titles stay hidden in Shortcuts. In version 1.0.10, once your iPhone has been locked for a few seconds, a shortcut that asks you to pick an article shows no titles until you unlock. Versions 1.0.4 to 1.0.9 showed them.

The Dig Deeper notification no longer names the article. It used to show the article’s new headline on your Lock Screen. Since November 12, 2026, it says “Your Dig Deeper is in” and “See what the sources say,” and nothing about what you’re reading. The panel that version 1.0.11 shows on your Lock Screen while a Dig Deeper runs doesn’t name the article either.

How we found them. AI agents build and review The Lede under the direction of its founder, Charles Klein, and he reviews what they produce. One of those agents reads our code the way an attacker would, and it found the first. The other two came out of our own checks of the app. Each change has an automated test that fails if it comes back.

What this doesn’t cover. Your iPhone’s own settings also decide what shows while it’s locked, such as notification previews, Siri, and the Siri Suggestions it learns from the Shortcuts actions you run. This note covers only what The Lede itself puts there.
